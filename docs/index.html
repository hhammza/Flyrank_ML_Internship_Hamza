Search visibility opportunities: an honest, human-reviewed ranking study

A transparent baseline and a small Logistic Regression model for deciding which visible, low-CTR pages deserve a closer look.

Hamza · Search visibility / CTR opportunity · September 2026

Abstract

This study asks whether decision-time search signals can help a content team rank pages for near-term review. I used the gated FlyRank warehouse's March 2026 daily performance partition, keeping client and content identifiers for grouping only and excluding future outcomes from the features. A Logistic Regression model used log impressions, log clicks, average position, and CTR, and was compared with the Week-4 transparent rule on the same client-held-out test rows. In the executed run, the model measured ROC-AUC 0.8458 and average precision 0.5224, while the rule's ROC-AUC was 0.5000 and average precision 0.1386; model precision@50 was 0.94. The output is decision support for human review, not proof that an edit causes more traffic or that a page definitely needs refreshing.

1. The question

Content teams cannot review every page every day. The question is: which client-content items look worth reviewing first because they have meaningful search visibility but unusually weak click-through? The output is a ranked queue; a human checks context before acting.

2. Data

The study uses the FlyRank internship warehouse release, specifically fact_content_daily_performance for March 2026. The model frame contains 2,229,796 daily rows from 45 clients after GSC availability and next-day-label construction. Features are measured on day t; the proxy label is whether the item receives a GSC click on the next observed day.

Hashes are grouping keys only. Future metrics, trend/label-derived fields, product flags, GA4 fields in this first model, and the sealed June sample are excluded. No client names, URLs, private queries, or raw content are shown.

The model is standardized Logistic Regression with seed 202609. Inputs are log_impressions, log_clicks, avg_position, and ctr. Client-grouped validation keeps every client entirely in train or test, asking whether the ranking transfers to unseen clients.

The Week-4 baseline flags impressions ≥500, positions 4–20, and CTR <0.5%. A deliberate label-copy attack reached a perfect score and was removed; this confirms the leakage harness works.

The model measured stronger ranking performance than the transparent rule on this held-out client split. The largest standardized coefficient was log_impressions, followed by average position and log clicks. This is a measured March result, not a guarantee for another period.

5. Limitations & honest framing

The next-day click indicator is a proxy, not an editorial ground truth. This observational data cannot establish that changing a title, snippet, or page causes more clicks. March is one development period; seasonality, SERP features, brand intent, availability, and client mix may change results. Grouped validation tests unseen clients, while time-aware validation is the stronger deployment check.

Safe claim: the model ranks items using decision-time signals associated with the observed next-day click proxy and can support human prioritization.

6. Ranked recommendations

  1. Review high-reach eligible pages first because reviewer time is limited.
  2. Check title and snippet alignment with search intent.
  3. Inspect brand context, SERP features, indexability, and recent changes.
  4. Record the decision and monitor the next period; do not claim lift from a single before/after comparison.

Never automate publishing, redirects, canonical or robots changes, deletion, or claims that an edit caused growth. These require qualified human review and, for causal conclusions, a stronger design.
